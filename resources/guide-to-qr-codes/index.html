Guide to QR Codes & NFC

Once you’ve created your DigiCon iD contact page, this guide shows you how to use QR codes and NFC tags so finders can reach you—whether on luggage, bags, or everyday items.

Easiest way to get value: scan our QR in an NFC app, then write to a tag

The QR codes and links we generate are designed to be scanned by NFC writing apps (such as NFC Tools by wakdev and NFC TagWriter by NXP on iOS and Android). Open the app, scan the QR code from My Account—or use the copied URL/vCard—and the app imports your contact page or vCard. Then tap your phone to an NFC tag to write it. No need to manually type anything on your phone. This is the simplest way to use DigiCon iD: generate once on the website, scan to import on your phone, then write to as many NFC tags as you like.

QR codes and your contact page

A QR (Quick Response) code is a 2D barcode that stores a URL or other data. When someone scans it with their phone camera or a QR app, they open that link—in your case, your DigiCon iD contact page with your details, destination, and how to reach you. No need to print your name or phone number on the tag; the code keeps your info digital and updatable.

Getting your QR codes from My Account

From My Account you can generate and download QR codes for each of your contact pages. You get three options:

  • Page URL — Opens your contact page in the browser (best for luggage and general use).
  • vCard — Adds your contact info directly into the finder’s phone (name, phone, email, etc.).
  • Plain text — A simple text version of your details for copying or sharing.

Use the Page URL QR for tags and stickers so finders land on your full contact page. Use the vCard QR when you want them to save your details straight to their contacts.

For NFC: These same QR codes are designed to be scanned inside NFC writing apps. The app reads the URL or vCard from the QR so you can write it to an NFC tag without entering anything by hand on your phone.

Step-by-step: printing and using your QR on luggage and belongings

  1. Sign in to My Account and open the contact page you want. Go to the QR Codes section (or generate QR during signup).
  2. Download or copy the QR image for “Page URL” (or vCard if you prefer). You can right‑click the QR to save the image, or use the download option if available.
  3. Choose your format: print the QR on a card (e.g. luggage tag, business-card size) or on sticker labels you can stick to bags, keys, or devices.
  4. Print on sturdy paper or cardstock. Use a clear, high-contrast print (black QR on white works best). If the tag will get wet or handled a lot, laminate the card or use a plastic sleeve.
  5. Attach to your items: thread a card through a luggage strap or loop, or peel and stick stickers onto suitcase handles, backpacks, or key rings. Place the QR where it’s easy to scan (flat and visible).

Durability tip: Laminate paper tags or use waterproof sticker material so the QR stays scannable after rain, handling, or long trips.

Using NFC tags instead of (or with) QR codes

NFC (Near Field Communication) lets a phone read or write small chips in tags, stickers, or cards by tapping. Unlike a QR code, the finder doesn’t need to open the camera—they tap their phone on the tag and can open your contact page or save your vCard. NFC works on most recent Android and iPhone models.

Types of NFC tags and where to use them

NFC comes in several forms; all can store a URL (your contact page) or vCard data:

  • NFC stickers — Thin, adhesive labels. Stick them on luggage, laptop, passport holder, or inside a bag.
  • NFC cards — Card-sized, like a business card. Good for wallets or as a luggage tag insert.
  • NFC keychains and luggage tags — Hard plastic or rubber. Durable for keys or bag straps.

What to write on the NFC tag

  • Contact page URL — Best for finders: one tap opens your full DigiCon iD page in the browser.
  • vCard — Writes your name, phone, email, etc. so the finder can save you as a contact with one tap.

No manual entry needed: Copy your contact page URL or vCard from My Account, or—even easier—scan the QR code from this website inside your NFC app. The app imports the URL or vCard so you can write it to the tag with one tap. You don’t have to type anything on your phone.

Apps to write NFC tags (iOS and Android)

Use an app that can write URLs or vCards to NFC tags—and that can scan a QR code to import that data. These work on both platforms:

NFC Tools (by wakdev) — Free app to read and write NFC tags. You can write a URL (your contact page) or create a contact/vCard. Available on iOS and Android.

NFC TagWriter by NXP — NXP’s official app for writing NFC tags. Supports writing URLs and contact (vCard) records. Available on iOS and Android.

Search for “NFC Tools” or “NFC TagWriter” in the App Store (iOS) or Google Play (Android). After installing, the easiest method is: open the app, use “Scan” or “Read QR” (or similar) to scan the QR code from your DigiCon iD page in My Account—the app imports the URL or vCard for you. Then choose “Write” and tap your phone to the NFC tag. No manual entry required.

Quick steps: putting your contact page on an NFC tag (no typing)

  1. In My Account, open your contact page and go to the QR Codes section. Have the Page URL or vCard QR visible on screen (or copy the URL).
  2. Install NFC Tools (wakdev) or NFC TagWriter by NXP on your iPhone or Android phone.
  3. In the app, use Scan QR (or “Add record” and then “From QR” / similar) and scan the QR code from the DigiCon iD page. The app imports the URL or vCard—no manual entry.
  4. Choose Write and tap your phone against the NFC tag. Once written, finders can tap the tag to open your page or save your contact.

Scanning QR codes

Most smartphones can scan QR codes with the built-in camera: open the camera, point it at the code, and tap the banner that appears. If that doesn’t work, install a free “QR code reader” or “QR scanner” app from the App Store or Google Play.
