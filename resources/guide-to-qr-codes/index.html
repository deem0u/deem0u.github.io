Guide to QR Codes & NFC

Once you’ve created your DigiCon iD contact page, this guide shows you how to use QR codes and NFC tags so finders can reach you—whether on luggage, bags, or everyday items.

Skip the typing: scan our QR in an NFC app, then tap to write

Our QR codes are meant to be scanned inside NFC apps like NFC Tools (wakdev) and NFC TagWriter by NXP (both iOS and Android). Open the app, scan the QR from My Account—or paste the URL if you’ve copied it—and the app pulls in your contact page or vCard. Then tap your phone to an NFC tag to write it. You don’t have to type a thing. Generate once on the site, scan on your phone, then write to as many tags as you need.

QR codes and your contact page

A QR (Quick Response) code is basically a 2D barcode that holds a link or other data. When someone scans it with their phone camera or a QR app, they get taken to that link—for you, that’s your DigiCon iD contact page with your details, destination, and how to reach you. You don’t have to print your name or number on the tag; the code does the job and you can update it anytime.

In My Account you get two options per contact page: Page URL (opens your contact page in the browser—best for tags and stickers) and vCard (saves your contact into the finder’s phone). Use Page URL for luggage and general use; use vCard when you want finders to save you as a contact. The same QR can be scanned inside an NFC app so you can write it to an NFC tag without typing anything on your phone.

Printing and using your QR

Sign in to My Account, open your contact page, and go to QR Codes. Download or copy the QR image (Page URL or vCard), then print it on a card (e.g. luggage tag) or sticker labels. Use sturdy paper or cardstock, black-on-white for best scan results; laminate or use a plastic sleeve if it’ll get wet. Attach the card to a strap or loop, or stick stickers on bags, keys, or devices—keep the QR flat and visible.

Tip: Laminate paper tags or use waterproof stickers so the QR still scans after rain or a beating.

Using NFC tags instead of (or with) QR codes

NFC (Near Field Communication) is the tech that lets your phone talk to little chips in tags, stickers, or cards by tapping. No camera needed—whoever finds your stuff just taps their phone on the tag and can open your contact page or save your vCard. Most recent Android and iPhone models support it.

NFC tags come as stickers, cards, or keychains—they all store a URL (your contact page) or vCard. Recommendation: if you want more on one tag (e.g. URL and vCard together), choose higher-capacity tags—NTAG216 is a good fit for this use, with enough storage for URL and vCard on one tag. Put the contact page URL on the tag so one tap opens your DigiCon iD page, or put a vCard so finders can save you as a contact. You don’t have to type: scan the QR from My Account inside an NFC app and the app pulls in the URL or vCard; then tap your phone to the tag to write it. Recommended apps: NFC Tools (wakdev) and NFC TagWriter by NXP (both free on iOS and Android). Search for them in the App Store or Google Play, open the app, scan the QR from your contact page, then choose Write and tap the tag. Done.

Scanning QR codes

Most phones can scan QR codes with the built-in camera: open the camera, point it at the code, and tap the link that pops up. If yours doesn’t, grab a free “QR code reader” or “QR scanner” app from the App Store or Google Play.
